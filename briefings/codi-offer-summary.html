Codi offer summaryBefore you sign

Five things to check before you accept

  1. The flexibility in the advert isn't in the contract. The job ad offered Cardiff, Newport or Swansea, hybrid working and a nine-day fortnight. The contract fixes your normal place of work at the Cardiff building, says nothing about hybrid, and clause 5.1 lets Codi move you permanently on one month's notice. Clause 6.1 also says flatly that you get no time off in lieu for extra hours — though the Schedule, which overrides it, does allow flexitime under policy. Get the hybrid arrangement and the nine-day fortnight confirmed in writing rather than relying on the advert.
  2. Pension isn't specified. Not in the offer letter, not in the Schedule, not in the Conditions — all three just say "rules on the intranet". No contribution rates anywhere. For a finance role this is the obvious thing to ask before signing.
  3. You need a car. Clause 5.4: you must hold a current licence and have access to a car insured for business use, and tell your manager immediately if either changes. No car allowance is mentioned, and clause 5.3 makes travel to your normal place of work your own cost.
  4. Sick pay doesn't start for six months. Statutory sick pay only until then, which is worth knowing if you're leaving a job where you're already past that threshold.
  5. Deductions are broad. Clause 7.4 lets Codi recover a list of things from your salary, including training and DBS costs if you leave during probation. And clause 30 reserves the right to make "reasonable changes" to your terms on notice.

One oddity to resolve today

The Schedule already carries "Signed by Sandra Vermaak — Date: 17 September 2026." Either you've already signed and returned it, or the template pre-fills your name. Worth checking which, because if it's pre-filled it would be easy to assume the acceptance is done when it isn't — and the seven days are running.

Three questions for Jennifer Eveleigh

1. What are the employer and employee pension contribution rates?

Ask for the scheme name and the rates in writing, not a link to the intranet you can't yet access.

2. Can you confirm the hybrid working pattern and the nine-day fortnight?

Both were in the advert. Neither is in the contract. A line in an email from her is enough.

3. What start date are you working towards?

It's "TBC" in the Schedule, and you'll need it to give notice. Your current notice period will drive this.

And then

None of the above is a reason not to take it. They're the normal gaps in a standard contract, and the pension question is the only one that could change the numbers materially. You reported to the panel chair you presented to, at the advertised salary, in the team you were pitching at. Congratulations — that's a good outcome.
